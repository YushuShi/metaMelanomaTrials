# Treatment vocabulary and R1 counting rules

The current treatment display/update authority is `config/treatment_registry.json`; see [Treatment normalization and updates](TREATMENT_NORMALIZATION.md). The initial vocabulary and R1 rules below are historical discovery context, not a separate current normalization dictionary.

The initial vocabulary covers relevant therapeutic families rather than a fixed number of drugs. It is an extensible search and normalization index, not an eligibility whitelist, an exhaustive inventory, or a statement that every listed agent currently has a particular approval. Generic IDs collapse explicit generic, salt/suffix, brand and development-code aliases. Unknown investigational agents go to an expansion queue and remain uncertain for review. No drug is excluded because it is absent from this starting index.

The local [metaOnco README](../../metaMeta/metaOnco/README.md) describes nutritional exposure and cancer-incidence data. Its nutritional classes are unsuitable as therapeutic drug classes. The contributing repositories' disease/source mappings are reused by the separate cancer-scope audit; their exposure requirements and incidence-only filters are not imported here. No source repository was edited.

The initial agent names/aliases were checked against [NCI's cancer-drug index](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/drugs/cancer-drugs) and [targeted-drug list](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/targeted-therapies/approved-drug-list), with explicit [checkpoint](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/immunotherapy/checkpoint-inhibitors) and [endocrine](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/hormone-therapy) classifications. The broad NCI targeted list includes endocrine agents and immunotherapies; its page heading is not used to erase their distinct operational classes. These sources were consulted on 2026-10-28. Each catalog entry lists its supporting source IDs; mechanism and target strings are normalized discovery annotations rather than patient-level treatment recommendations.

Endocrine agents include ADT/ovarian suppression and additional androgen-pathway agents. Two endocrine agents do not become endocrine plus nonendocrine targeted therapy. Shared anticancer backbones contribute to scientific regimen composition but retain their background role for financial attribution. Supportive steroids are recorded separately. Chemotherapy limited to the comparator, prior therapy or subsequent therapy does not downgrade the experimental comparison.

ADCs are single distinct targeted agents, with target and payload recorded. Their payload does not alone establish concurrent conventional chemotherapy. The catalog keeps unconjugated targeted antibodies and checkpoint antibodies distinct. For example, the [FDA mirvetuximab review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/761310Orig1s000ChemR.pdf) identifies its FR-alpha targeting and DM4 payload; [PADCEV labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=b5631d3e-4604-4363-8f20-11dfc5a4a8ed) documents enfortumab plus pembrolizumab in EV-302 and preserves its chemotherapy comparator options.

The regimen catalog contains actual trial examples: CheckMate 067, RELATIVITY-047, KEYNOTE-426, COMBI-d, PALOMA-2, PROpel and EV-302, each linked to an original report or authoritative trial description. It does not create hypothetical combinations. A comparator agent list can represent alternative arms/options rather than a concurrent regimen. Exact doses, sequence, shared backbones and comparator arms remain report-specific.

R1 uses the frozen prompt and output schema in `config/prompts/`. The postprocessor requires positive, applicable, source-grounded evidence for exclusions. Missing abstracts are uncertain, not exclusions. Original subgroup, follow-up, safety, biomarker and pooled trial-patient analyses remain potentially eligible. Published literature meta-analyses/reviews are distinct from original pooled participant analyses. Registry-only records remain in a separate review collection and are not counted as published articles. Missing numeric effects, denominators, trial IDs and endpoint details are not automatic exclusions.

All comparison records survive normalization. For a counting headline, resolved core comparisons have equal precedence; then deferred experimental concurrent cytotoxic regimens, endocrine-only separate comparisons, and unresolved review comparisons follow. Lexical source-order `comparison_id` breaks ties; it never favors endocrine combinations or a cancer site. Secondary disease, comparison, regimen, endpoint and subgroup tags are nonadditive. The chosen headline supplies the default comparison-specific drug attribution, while all comparator/background roles remain available. R1 counts are preliminary workload counts, not final eligible-study, cohort or participant counts.

A successful HTTP/model request does not prove every batch report was processed. `postprocess_batch` marks omitted model results `model_result_present=false` and `processing_status=unprocessed`; they remain uncertain in the missing-results queue. Only actual returned result IDs may be confirmed as processed in the ledger. No second LLM round is triggered by this module. The execution controller must stop at `STOPPED_AFTER_R1_AWAITING_USER_REVIEW`.
